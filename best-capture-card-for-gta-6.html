Best Capture Card for GTA 6

Short answer

For most players recording or streaming GTA 6 gameplay, an external USB capture card with 4K60 HDR passthrough like the Elgato HD60 X offers the best balance of quality, compatibility, and easy setup. Step up to an internal PCIe card like the Elgato 4K60 Pro MK.2 only if you need the highest-bitrate capture for heavy editing, or down to a budget pick if you mainly want quick highlight clips.

Grand Theft Auto VI is shaping up to be the most visually ambitious entry in the series, and plenty of players already plan to capture their first runs through Vice City for streams, montages, or simple personal archives. Rockstar Games has not published official PC requirements or confirmed any streaming-specific features for GTA 6, so treat any claims about exact resolutions, frame rates, or capture modes as unconfirmed until Rockstar states otherwise. What hasn't changed is the underlying hardware math: pulling smooth, high-resolution footage off a console or a second gaming PC without dragging down your main rig's frame rate still calls for a dedicated capture card with its own encoder. Below we compare three current capture cards — one built for uncompressed quality, one built for everyday streaming convenience, and one built for a tight budget — across the specs that actually affect capture quality and on-screen delay.

How we chose these capture cards

We prioritized cards with mature driver support, dedicated hardware encoding — so your CPU isn't doing double duty while GTA 6 renders a dense, traffic-filled city — and clean HDR passthrough, since Vice City's day-to-night lighting shifts are the kind of scene that benefits from accurate on-screen color while you play. We also weighted ease of setup, since not everyone wants to crack open a desktop case to seat an internal card. Premium pick: Elgato 4K60 Pro MK.2

The 4K60 Pro MK.2 is an internal PCIe card, which means it skips the extra USB bottleneck and can push the highest-bitrate footage of the three picks here. It's the right call if you're planning to edit cinematic GTA 6 footage at full resolution rather than just clip highlights.

Setup tips for recording GTA 6 gameplay

Whichever card you pick, match your output settings to what the card can actually handle before you start recording — pushing a console or PC to a resolution or HDR mode the card doesn't support usually just drops the passthrough image instead of upgrading the capture. Use a wired HDMI connection between your console or PC and the capture card rather than any wireless workaround, since compression artifacts and latency both get worse over wireless links. If you're capturing from a PC, keep the capture software and GPU drivers current, since encoder compatibility tends to improve with firmware and driver updates over time. Because Rockstar hasn't detailed GTA 6's PC display options or any built-in capture tools, it's worth revisiting your settings once official specs are confirmed rather than assuming today's guidance will carry over exactly.

Do you even need a dedicated capture card?

Not always. Current consoles already include built-in share and record features that are fine for quick clips, and PC players with a capable GPU can lean on built-in recording tools from their graphics card vendor instead of adding hardware. A dedicated capture card earns its keep when you want a lag-free live preview while recording, need to capture from a source with no built-in recorder, or want footage encoded independently so it doesn't compete with GTA 6 itself for GPU resources. If your plans for Vice City footage are casual, start with what you already own; if you're building a channel around it, the picks above are a reasonable next step.
