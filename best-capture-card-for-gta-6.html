What to look for in a capture card for GTA 6

Four things decide whether a capture card is worth buying for a game like this: passthrough resolution (what you see on your monitor while capturing), capture resolution (what actually gets recorded), interface bandwidth, and whether encoding happens on the card itself or eats into your CPU. Internal PCIe cards remove almost all of that CPU overhead, external USB cards trade a little headroom for painless setup on a laptop or console, and budget models push more of the encoding work onto your computer — fine for 1080p, tighter for anything higher.

Setup tips for smooth 4K capture

A few habits make a bigger difference than the card itself. Capture to a fast internal SSD rather than an external spinning drive so write speeds don't stutter during long GTA 6 sessions. Keep your capture software's bitrate and frame rate matched to your card's real capture resolution rather than pushing it past spec. And leave a generation of headroom on your CPU or GPU encoder if you're streaming and gaming on a single PC, since GTA 6's demanding visuals will already be using most of what you have.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a capture card if I'm on console? Not always — many consoles have built-in capture and streaming tools, but an external card like the HD60 X gives you more control over quality, overlays, and multi-source streaming setups than the built-in options.

Will GTA 6 need a specific capture card? No — Rockstar has not announced any capture-card partnership or requirement for GTA 6, so any card that meets the resolution and passthrough specs above will work fine.
